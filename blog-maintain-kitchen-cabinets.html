MAINTENANCE GUIDE

How to Maintain Your Newly Fitted Kitchen Cabinets

New cabinetry deserves simple, consistent care. The right routine protects the finish, hardware and moving parts while keeping your kitchen looking fresh.

The golden rule: clean gently, dry thoroughly and deal with spills or moisture promptly.

1. Start with a gentle cleaning routine

Use a soft, lint-free cloth and a mild, non-abrasive cleaner suitable for your cabinet finish. Avoid soaking doors or allowing water to sit around edges and joints.

2. Deal with spills and moisture promptly

Kitchen cabinets regularly encounter water, oils and food spills. Wipe them away as soon as practical, especially around sinks, dishwashers and worktops.

3. Protect the cabinet finish

Avoid abrasive pads, harsh solvents, aggressive scouring powders and excessive scrubbing. Test any unfamiliar cleaning product on an inconspicuous area first.

4. Give hinges and runners some attention

Open and close doors and drawers periodically to check for stiffness, looseness or alignment issues. If your hardware manufacturer specifies a lubricant or adjustment procedure, follow those instructions.

5. Keep heat and steam under control

Use extractor ventilation where appropriate and avoid directing prolonged heat or steam at cabinet doors, particularly around ovens, kettles and dishwashers.

6. Keep heavy items where the cabinet was designed to carry them

Distribute weight sensibly and avoid overloading drawers, shelves or specialised hardware. Heavy cookware is usually best stored in areas designed for it.

7. Inspect your kitchen every few months

Check edges, hinges, handles, runners, seals and areas around plumbing. Early attention to a loose hinge or small moisture issue can prevent a bigger repair later.

A simple maintenance schedule

Daily: wipe spills and moisture. Weekly: clean doors, handles and frequently touched surfaces. Monthly: check hinges, runners and plumbing-adjacent areas. Every 6–12 months: review alignment, hardware and areas exposed to heat or moisture.

What should you never do?

Do not flood cabinet surfaces with water, use abrasive cleaning materials, leave spills standing, hang excessive weight from doors, or ignore swelling, damaged edging or persistent moisture.

A well-designed kitchen is meant to be lived in. With a simple care routine, your cabinetry can remain practical, beautiful and enjoyable for years.
